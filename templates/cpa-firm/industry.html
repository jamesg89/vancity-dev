We understand your numbers

Generic bookkeepers treat a contractor like a retail shop. These are the five things that actually go wrong.

Job costing that nobody maintains

Materials coded to one bucket, labour to another, and no way to tell which jobs made money. We set up classes or projects so every job carries its own cost and margin — and we keep them current.

1099 subs and missing W-9s

You found a great framer in June and nobody collected a W-9. We track subcontractor payments against documentation all year, so January is a filing exercise, not a scramble.

WIP, deposits and retainage

Customer deposits booked as revenue make a profitable month that isn’t. We handle work-in-progress and over/under-billing properly, which is also what a bonding agent or lender wants to see.

Equipment, trucks and Section 179

Buying the excavator in December versus January can be a five-figure difference. We model it before you sign, and we keep a fixed-asset schedule that matches the depreciation on the return.

Cash in, cash gone

Progress billings, 60-day receivables and payroll every Friday. We build a simple 13-week cash view so you know whether you can take the next job.

Sales tax on labour vs. materials

{{STATE}} treats them differently, and getting it wrong compounds quietly for years. We set the rules up once, then check the liability accounts tie to what was filed.

{{INDUSTRY_NAME}}: common questions

Do you work in {{SOFTWARE_1}} with job costing turned on?

Yes — and if your file was set up without projects or classes, fixing that is part of onboarding. We also work in {{SOFTWARE_2}} and integrate the common trades apps for estimating and time tracking.

Can you handle certified payroll and prevailing wage?

Yes, for public work in {{STATE}}. We’ll need your wage determinations and fringe rates; we produce the weekly reports and keep the records in case of an audit.

Should I be an S-corp?

Often, once profit is consistently above roughly $60–80k, but not always — it depends on reasonable salary, health insurance, retirement contributions and whether you have partners. We run the numbers both ways before recommending it, and we tell you when the answer is no.

My last accountant never asked about my jobs. Is that normal?

It’s common, and it’s why owners end up guessing. A compliance-only accountant files an accurate return from bad data. Job-level reporting is what tells you to stop bidding the work that loses money.
